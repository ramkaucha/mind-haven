The asymptotic growth of functions fall into several categories, the ones listed below are functions that arise commonly in the analysis of algorithms and grow no slower than constant, as supposed to decaying functions such as $\frac{1}{x}$ , $-2x$ and $e^{-x}$

- *Constant functions* (e.g. $f(n) = 1$) don't grow at all
- *Logarithmic functions* (e.g. $f(n) = log_2n$) grow slowly towards infinity
- Polynomial functions (e.g. $f(n) = n^3 + 4$) grow faster the higher their degree. All polynomials grow faster than all logarithms.
- Exponential functions (e.g. $f(n) = 2^n$) grow faster the larger their base. All exponentials grow faster than all polynomials.
