
# Calculating personal income tax
1. Assessable Income
	Any income that tax legislation deems to be 'assessable'
		Salary or wages
		benefits provided employer
		interest, dividends
2. Allowable deductions
	Expenses that you have incurred while generating assessable income that are 'allowed' by tax legislation and haven't been reimbursed by your employer
		work-related training and education
		work-related travel or clothing expenses
		interest or other necessary expenses related to investment income
		donations to tax deductible charities (not places of worship in Australia)
3. Taxable income
	Assessable income less Allowable deductions
	a key number in our calculations
4. Calculate tax on taxable income using the tax table
5. Add levies
	additional taxes may apply to taxable income or the components of assessable income
	Aus, there is a 2% medicare levy on taxable income to partly cover government healthcare
6. Add others taxes
	additional taxes may apply to various investments
	e.g. 15% tax on dividends received on any shares
7. Add payments to government
	e.g. student loan scheme
8. Less tax rebates or offsets
	tax rebates are very different from allowable deductions
	they are usually associated with tax on investment income that has already been paid
9. Calculate personal tax income
	tax on taxable income + levies and taxes + repayments - tax rebates
10. Deduct tax already paid
11. Calculate Net Tax Payment

# Preparing a tax return
Three options:
1. Do it yourself using myTax (online)
	A good system for those with a reasonable simple situation and financial intelligence
	Most of the information is pre-filled by the ATO
2. Tax Agent (cheaper)
	Need a license but may not have a university degree
	tax return preparation fees are an allowable deductions
3. Accountant (more expensive)
	University degree and member of professional accounting organisations (CPA or CA ANZ)
	tax return preparation fees are an allowable deduction

## Section of a tax return
income - to calculate assessable income
deductions - to calculate allowable deductions
losses carried forward
tax offsets
private health insurance
medicare levy issues
various adjustments
supplementary sections for some investments

**Helpful to have before starting**
Copy of last year's income tax assessment
TFN
final payslip for the financial year from employer with total payments
Centrelink summary statements
your june bank statement that summarises interest and fees for the year
receipts for any deductions
tax summaries for any managed funds or other investments
buys, sells and dividends for any shares

## Tax return tips
keep a special tax folder during the year and put any relevant documents in it as you receive them
make sure you have everything before you start your tax return
Keep all records for 5 years

## When to get help
Get an accountant (or tax agent) if you have
	own business
	investment properties
	depreciation calculations
	lots of allowable deductions
	a 'questionnaire' from the ATO asking you how you derived your calculations

# Personal income tax strategies

**Allowable deductions**
ATO gives a checklist for deductions, search it up

**Income Splitting**
For people who are married or in long-term defacto relationship
generally used when one spouse is at home looking after children while other is doing paid work
worker's taxable income is $80,000
Carer's taxable income is $15,000
All savings and investments should be placed under carer's name to take advantage of their tax-free threshold and lower marginal tax rate
Self-employed or family-businesses use this as well, carer works as book-keeper in return for legitimate salary

**Income timing**
income is usually taxed when it is paid
sometimes you have a choice when to be paid, (e.g. bonus, contract work), 
if you expect more money next FY, try to get paid before June 30, or vise versa. (July 1)

**Beware of**
Complex personal finances
	added accounting and financial planning fees
	increased personal time
	increase 'switching costs' to change structure
	decreased happiness because of time and hassle
Adequate records
	you must keep detailed records of deductions for 5 years

# Interest
Interest on bank accounts is treated as ordinary assessable income
# Gains in value
Any gains in value of investment (such as property or shares) are treated as 'assessable income'
If you held the investment for more than 1 year then only 50% of the gain is 'assessable income'
primary residence is exempt from tax on gains if it has not been rented out to tenants
e.g. 
John buys some shares for $5,000 and then sells them 2 years later for $7,000. Assessable income of $2,000 x 0.5 = $1,000 is recorded in the tax year in which he sold the shares

Under the '6 year rule'  it may still be possible to temporarily live away from your primary residence, rent it out and the capital gains may still be exempt
	however, this is 'grey area' in tax law

# Rent from property
rent income on property is assessable income, split between owners of property
there can be lot of allowable deductions
	interest on an investment loan used to purchase the property
	maintenance
	agent fees
	depreciation on fixtures
gains on primary residence are tax free
# Dividends from shares
When companies earn profits they pay some of those profits to shareholders in the form of 'dividends', which are taxed at rate of 15%.
They are taxed at a reduced rate since the company has already paid corporate tax on profits

Most governments 'double dip' with taxation
	companies pay tax on their profits (30%)
	small businesses with turnover less than $50 million pay only 25% corporate tax rate
	Dividends are paid to shareholders out of after-tax profits
	Individual pay tax on their dividends (up to 47% tax)
Australia has stopped this through 'franked dividends'
A fully-franked dividends consists of a cash payment and also some 'franking credits' (tax credits)
Government refunds any corporate tax paid by the company to the individual shareholder as tax rebate
Add the total of the cash amount plus the franking credits to your assessable income
