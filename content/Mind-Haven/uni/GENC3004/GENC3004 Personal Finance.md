**Review**
This course was taken in Summer Term 2025. In consisted for 9 topics surrounding personal finance. The topics were pretty generalised which is expected. However, the examples and plans provided in the lectures were close to what I plan to do myself.

Being in Summer Term, the topics were all smashed in together, which meant to there was like 8 hours worth of lectures to watch, which is pretty challenging. However, these lectures were provided way before the summer term even began, so you can possibly start way earlier and make it easy on yourself (which I didn't).

Having a somewhat idea of personal finance, I would say the course still allowed me to learn more, and better my knowledge on topics that I previously didn't understand, especially [[Risk Management]].

The assessments were pretty big, with writing over 15,000 words between the 3 assignments, which to me is a lot since I haven't written paragraphs since high school. But, I would say the assignments were very helpful, and I learnt a lot. Especially, the financial plan, I think it's a document that will be useful to me even after graduating.


Below are the topics we went through:


Personal finance is the practice of managing your money and resources to achieve your life goals. Creating a careful plan and making informed decisions about how to earn, spend and grow your wealth over time.

# [[Financial Intelligence]] <br />
The skill of turning financial information into meaningful insights for better decision-making. The topic involves understanding your current financial position, setting future goals, identifying gaps, developing strategies, managing risks and taking actions through planning of income, expenses, and investments.


# [[Financial Strategy]] <br />
Financial strategy starts with the understanding the complex relationship between money and happiness, because understanding their relationship is crucial for making effective financial decisions. The topic goes into the predictability of irrational decisions made by humans, prospect theory, utility theory, different types of money worshipers, understanding your money personality, the values and goal settings, investment and returns and financial calculations.

# [[Financial Independence]] <br />
Financial independence is the situation in which the income you are drawing from your investments is enough to cover your living expenses.  The topic covers FIRE, variants of FIRE, the common strategies, the superannuation system and everything that surrounds super.

# [[Career Strategy]] <br />
Understanding your career, your career goals, the economy, how to choose a career, and developing your career strategy.

[[Property and Loans]] <br />
Covers the property and loans around Sydney. The good and bad, different types of property taxes, what to do before, during and after purchasing a property, the loans regarding property, loan features, financial leverage and home loan tips.

# [[Risk Management]] <br />
Understanding risks, expected value and risk visualised, fair premium, the logic of insurance, risk and human bias, MEAT, Risk pooling, different types of insurance, estate planning, divorce.

# [[Taxation Planning]]<br />
Taxation planning is all about how to get the most out of your tax returns, how to calculate personal income tax, personal income tax rates, preparing a tax return, income tax strategies, interest, gains in value, rent from property, dividends from shares, leveraged investments, investment tax strategies.

# [[Investing in Shares]]<br />
Covers the understanding of valuing shares, investment risks, portfolio theory, investing in shares, different types of shares in Australia, non-mainstream investments (to avoid or be careful)

# [[Investment Strategy]]<br />
Covers the topics of margin loans, LVR, Longevity and sequencing risks, risk profile, asset allocations, deciding on strategic asset allocations and financial and investment strategy, also provides scenarios.
