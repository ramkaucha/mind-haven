### Risk-aversion
Not willing to take risk unless there is an adequate reward for doing so

Risk-averse - A is preferred to B
Risk-neutral - No preference between A and B
Risk-seeking - B is preferred to A

Risk-averse people could prefer either A or C to B
![[Pasted image 20250118202239.png]]
A is preferred to B - Lower risk for same expected value
C is preferred to B - Higher expected value for same risk
No clear preference for A or C - Lower risk-aversion may prefer C, higher risk-aversion may prefer A

### The logic of insurance
A person who is risk-averse (rational), should remove pure risk (downside risk) by paying a regular premium as long as that premium is 'reasonable'

Asymmetric information 
	The person being insured and the insurance company have different information 
	The person being insured does not want to pass on negative information about themselves 
	This can make it hard for the insurance company to determine an appropriate premium 
Adverse selection 
	All the ‘low-risk’ people are not willing to pay the insurance premium
	Only the ‘high-risk’ people are willing to pay the insurance premium 
	Providing insurance in this case may not be possible (market failure) 
Moral hazard
	Someone who has taken out insurance transfers risk to the insurance company 
	They may have little or no incentive to mitigate or avoid the risk (bad behaviour) 
	Insurance provider may need to monitor or provide incentives to encourage good behaviour

### MEAT the risk-management alternatives
1. Mitigate the risk - take steps to reduce the probability and/or impact of the bad outcome
2. Eliminate the risk - Remove yourself from the risky situation completely
3. Accept the risk - Set aside some funds (financial slack) to effectively 'self-insure'
4. Transfer the risk, by paying the premium - Buy insurance, transfer or share the risk with a third-party

### Risk management steps
1. Identify risks
	What can go wrong with each component of your financial and investment strategy
	What can go wrong with your career strategy or health
2. Visualise and document the risk
	Visualise in detail how the risk could 'play out' and document it
	Are there links between different risks that could cause a cascade
3. Evaluate the risk
	What is the likelihood (probability) and impact (severity) of each risk?
4. Identify how to MEAT the challenges of each risk
	Mitigate, eliminate, accept or transfer the risk
5. Take action now and then monitor

Many people do not manage risks well

They don't spend time identifying and planning for risks
They do not maintain a cash buffer or financial slack
They fail to take steps to look after physical and mental health
They fail to continuously train to keep employment skills up-to-date
Thy are too inflexible after losing a job
They maintain too high operating leverage (too many fixed costs)
They don't adequately diversify 
They don't get ahead on their home loan payments
Too financially leverage (too much debt and interest expenses)

## Risk Pooling

Scenario:
There are 10 houses in a street They all worry about being burgled … but let us assume that there is no insurance available Each house has the same chance of being burgled The expected amount stolen in a burglary is $10,000 On average, 1 house is burgled per year 10% probability of being burgled each year … with expected cost of 0.1 x 10,000 = $1,000 per year

1. Mitigate - add security systems, barred windows, remove automatic garage doors
2. Eliminate - add fences, and wires, high-security doors, dogs, security guards
3. Accept - save some financial slack to allow for stolen (self-insurance)
4. Transfer - transfer or share the risk with others via risk-pooling
	Every household puts $1,000 into a central account each year
	They are required to take 'reasonable' actions to mitigate risk
	Any house burgled in any given year can claim from funds in the 'pool'
		If there are no burglaries, then the pool is 'rolled over' into the following year
		if there is not enough money to claims then bad luck

It can be better to transfer risk when
1. impact of the event is high
2. probability of event occurring is low
3. premiums are reasonable given the impact and probability
	a 'premium' is the monthly payment you pay to the insurance company

Better to accept risk when its the opposite

Shared pain via a deductible
Insurance companies want you to behave in a 'low-risk' way
they make sure it 'costs you' if the event happens
You must pay a 'deductible' or 'excess' if the insured event occurs
e.g. you pay first $500 of expenses if motor accident is your fault, you pay first $1,000 of medical expenses for an elective surgery

### Screening
Insurance companies don't want 'bad apples' in a risk-pool
Each person completes detailed applications forms
If you are 'high probability' of experiencing 'event' they
1. Won't provide you with insurance or
2. add an 'exclusion' clause to your policy or
3. Charge a ridiculously high premium

### Signalling
Applicants need to 'signal' they are low-risk and honest
Complete the detailed application forms accurately
If dishonest;
1. they may still collect your premiums
2. your claim is rejected and no payout occurs
3. you go onto 'black list' and can't get insurance

Insurance is not a 'discount club'.
Risk pooling doesn't work this way:
1. you are high risk so excluded from risk pool
2. How can a risk-pool work if everyone does this?
3. Insurance is not just about 'the dollars' its about peace of mind
## Life Insurance

**Death insurance**
Pays lump sum when you die, also called 'term insurance', used by family to pay-off debts and cover future living expenses, cheapest way to get it is via superannuation account, calculate based on needs: `total debt - investments - superannuation + PV of replacement income`
Example:
John and Sarah are a couple aged 30 with 2 dependent children They have a home loan of $500,000 They each have $100,000 in superannuation accounts They have $50,000 of other investments If either die, they would need $50,000 per year for 30 years (until age 60) The real rate of return on investments is 5% per annum

Cover = debt -investments - super + $C \times [\frac{1-(1+r)^{-n}}{r}]$
=500,000 - 50,000 - 100,000 + $50000 \times [\frac{1-1.05^{-30}}{0.05}]$
=1,118,623

**Total & Permanent Disability**
Pays a lump sum if you become very disabled
It is an 'add-on' when you take out death loan e.g. $1 million death cover + TPD cover
Used for same purpose as death cover, proving death is easy with 'death certificate', but proving totally and permanently disability is difficult

**Income Protection**
Replaces up to 90% (first six months) then 70% (subsequent periods) of your salary if you have accident, also called 'salary continuance' and 'Income Insurance'

**Workers Compensation**
Type of basic 'income protection' paid by your employer, safety-net for people without income protection or savings, replaces income and pays some medical expenses.

**Trauma**
Pays a lump sum amount (e.g. $100,000) when you suffer a serious medical condition, used for large medical bills, house renovation and care, cannot purchase through superannuation, not usually necessary if you have good private health insurance.

## Life Insurance Tips
**When young and single**

2. Accept the risk of small losses
3. Consider choosing higher excesses to save on premiums

## Wills, choices and tips

### Will
Legal document with clear instructions about what will happen after you die
Who will rec3eive your stuff (beneficiaries)
	real assets (car, music, computers)
	financial assets (bank accounts and investments)
	until distributed, they are owned by 'the estate of john smith'
who will be guardian of your children
who will oversee the whole process (executor)
with adequate 'backup' clauses in case beneficiaries or executors die before you do (predecease you)

**Why have a valid will**
Banks and financial institutions don't just 'hand over' ownership of your stuff after you die
A will makes transferring title of your financial assets easier for your family
This is important since they will also be grieving
A will reduces the likelihood of family arguments
A good will reduces admin and legal costs
If you are married and don't have one then the laws of intestacy end up screwing your family

**What can't be covered in will**
Any assets owned jointly with someone else
Assets that you do not direct own
	Super is owned by a trust on your behalf and so you cannot give instructions about it in your will

### Will Choices

**Choosing distribution**
Normally a bad idea to leave specific assets to people because you may not have them when you die or it may be unfair
Better to say 50% of all assets to this person, 25% to another, 25% to another and let them decide what assets they want
Make sure there is a residual beneficiary in case everyone else is dead 

If single, usually leave 100% to either siblings or parents
If married, usually leave 100% to your spouse
However, need to also leave instructions for if your spouse or members of your family are dead
For example:
	I leave 100% to my spouse
	If spouse predeceases me then 100% to my sister,
	If sister predeceases me then 100% to my parents,

**Choosing an executor**
Executor overseas the whole process of distributing assets in conjunction with a solicitor
Qualities of an executor:
	Someone who 'gives a stuff'
	Usually a close family member (parent or sibling)
	Good administrator and follow-through skills

**Guardianship of children**
Applicable if both you and your spouse ide
Particularly important part of your will
Need to consider their financial strength, ability to manage, philosophy, religious views and willingness.
nominated guardians may refuse

**Signing the will**
if incorrectly executed (signed) then it is invalid
You must sign the document
Your signature 'witnessed' by two people
Witnesses cannot be beneficiaries (e.g. your family)
Preferably all to sign with the same pen
Best for all to initial each page of the will (if > 1 page)
Best to then take copies and provide to executor, beneficiaries, guardian and family solicitor

### Will Tips
Keep it flexible for your beneficiaries
Remember that your situation changes frequently and so try to keep your will fairly general so that it does not data quickly
Try to draft your will so that it only needs updating once every 10 years or so
Sign several copies and leave them with the executor and various family members

**Alternatives for preparing one**
1. Will kit (online or offline)
2. Solicitor (lawyer)

**Storage of Wills**
Make certified copies (certified by Justice of Peace or Solicitor)
Provide a copy to the Executor
	Attach a note that informs them where the original is kept
You can keep the original with your solicitor for a fee

## Enduring Power of Attorney
If married or aged, consider arranging Enduring Power of Attorney with the solicitor when drafting your will
'Power of attorney' allows them to buy and sell your investments on while you are still 'mentally capable'
	Good for temporarily granting permission if you are overseas a lot and cannot sign documents.
'Enduring Power of Attorney' allows them to buy and sell investments even if you are mentally incapable

### Enduring Guardianship
Normal enduring power of attorney only handles assets
An Enduring Guardian authorises another person to make decisions about your health and lifestyle in the event that you cannot make these decisions yourself.
They can only be authorised when the person is of sound mind. Best to prepare one with a solicitor.

## Divorce - Binding Financial Agreements (BFA) 
Prenuptial agreements

**Divorce often adversely affects finances**
Loss of income (or a reduction in the growth rate in future income)
	Long-term effects on mental health
	Increased care responsibilities for dependants
	Additional time spent on household chores and life admin
Increase in expenses
	Total expenses increase when one household becomes two households
	Additional care expenses for dependants
	Legal expenses associated with the divorce
Loss in assets and wealth
	Property settlement splits assets and liabilities according to equity

**Property settlement upon divorce**
Property settlement and divorce are two different legal processes
	Property settlement is the formal division of property following a couple separating
Australia is an 'equitable distribution country'
	Wealth is not split evenly
Process:
1. Value the assets (and liabilities)
2. Value the contributions of each party before, during and after relationship
3. Calculate future needs
4. Consider the practical effect of the property settlement
Most divisions are 55 to 65% in favour of economically weaker spouse

### Binding Financial Agreements (BFS)
also known as prenuptial agreement
governs your property interests in the event of a separation or divorce
	can apply to married or de facto relationships
alters the normal avenue for division of property
can help to build alignment around finances early in relationship
the agreement must be in writing and signed by both parties - each party must enter into it of their own free will and be informed
each party must receive independent legal advice
the cast of engaging a lawyer and drawing up BFA starts at ~$3,000
	it can be significantly more expensive than this given that you need separate lawyers
## Property Insurance

### Home Insurance
Insures your home against calamities
	floods and wildfire are sometimes not covered
does not cover the value of your land
for units - paid through strata
most people underinsured by 30% plus
amount should cover:
	demolition of damaged structure
	Architect and council fees
	Rebuild similar home (new)
	Costs of temporary accommodation elsewhere
	Generally, add 20% to cost of rebuilding

### Contents Insurance
Protects the contents of your house (furniture) against calamities and theft
You should keep receipts, photographs of items and an asset register to provide evidence in case of a claim
Watch out for under-insuring devices and clothes
Insuring jewellery is difficult
Public liability included with contents - other people suing you for negligence

### Mortgage Insurance
Lender's Mortgage Insurance (LMI)
	Protects the lender if you are borrowing more than 80% of the value of a property in case you can't make the payments
	Does not protect you
	Paid as lump-sum when you take out the loan
Mortgage Protection Insurance
	Pays your mortgage if you have an accident or illness that prevents you from working
	Protects you
	Better to have proper income protection insurance !

### Property Insurance Tips
1. Don't underinsure
2. Go home and ask your parent show much they are covered for home and contents insurance - when did they last update these amounts, have they got an asset register with evidence of contents
3. Premiums not proportional to the amount being insurance - sometimes double the cover only adds 10% to the premium
4. Choose higher excesses if possible and save the excess into a special savings account
## Motor Vehicle Insurance
### Third party personal injury insurance
Covers you against personal injury of anyone you hit/hurt while driving your car
	CTP Insurance or Green Slip in Australia
Usually required when you renew registration each year
Purchase from insurance companies
Provides 'peace-of-mind' that at least your medical expenses will be covered if you are hit by another driver

### Third Party Property Insurance
Protects you if you damage some else's property
	$100,000 BMW or $20,000 fence
	$500,000 in lost earnings for a shape facade
Watch out for 'driver substitution'
	Take out insurance under parent's name as primary driver, but you are the one involved in the accident
This is a necessary cost of running a car
	if you can't afford it then don't buy the car in the first place
	they ask about accidents, speeding fines and other driving history information in your application
	they check it when you make a claim

### Comprehensive Insurance
Covers property damage to your car as well
covered for 'agreed value' or 'market value'
excess designed to deter small claims
car required to be 'free of damage'
no claims bonus (discount) of up to 60%
premiums are often priced to be unaffordable if you don't have maximum no-claims bonus
Premiums vary significantly by suburb and how garaged
Some insurers will offer you full no-claim bonus status if your parents are good customers

### Motor Vehicle Insurance tips
1. Consider buying very inexpensive, second-hand car after graduation and only take out 3rd party property insurance
2. Can sometimes save if you have home and contents insurance with insurance company
## Health Insurance

### Private Health Insurance covers
Hospital covers
	Medicare gap - difference between what medicare will cover and scheduled fees
	Medical gap - difference between scheduled fees and what the doctor charges (in selected hospitals only)
general extras - dental, optical, physio, expenses, funds cover maximum of $X per visit and $Y per year
Ambulance costs - the costs of emergency transport by ambulance (expensive!)

### Deductibles, excesses and co-payments
the part of any medical costs that you have to pay
designed to discourage hypochondriac behaviour
sometimes based on 'days' in hospital, e.g. HCF is $50 per year spent in hospital
sometimes based on 'total per year'.
the higher the deductible, excess or co-payment the lower the premiums
best to shave 'savings buffer' and choose higher excess but lower the excess 12 months before having a baby

### Health Insurance tips
remember that insurance is designed to protect you against worst-case scenarios
most people lose on extras cover over the long term, cheaper to have hospital cover only and save the difference
research and consider each fund carefully, iselect.com.au provides some okay guidance
consider 'hospital only cover' with high excess, self-insure for general extras unless it is definitely worth it
always get ambulance cover

an absolute 'must-have' when travelling
