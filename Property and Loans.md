
# Property

## Good, Bad and the Ugly

**The good**
1. Control over basic needs - Provides shelter for your family and also financial and emotional security
2. Control over dwelling expenses - Especially once home loan is paid off, no rent or mortgage payments 
3. Option to make improvements - Renovation to suit your needs, tastes, children and/or to improve valuation
4. Stability in lifestyle - no need to worry about a landlord asking you to leave or selling the property
5. General increase in price - Prices are expected to increase over the long-term in line with nominal income growth
6. Main residence capital gains tax exemption - any gains in your main residence not assessable for income tax
7. Financial leverage magnifies return on equity - if the return on the asset exceeds the interest rate on debt, it magnifies return on equity
8. Cheap interest rates - Residential property is a special form of collateral. Banks are able to lend at low rates
9. Tangible - Shares and other investments are intangible. It is nice to be able to see your asset
10. Experience - Typical 25-year-old can better assess quality of a property than the quality of a share
11. Forces saving disciplines
12. Low levels of market efficiency - Opportunities to profit from uninformed or biased buyers or sellers
13. Excluded from asset tests for social security - main residence is not included in asset tests for social security, such as age pension
14. Source of funds for retirement

**The Bad**
1. Non-divisibility - cannot hand part of a property
2. Poor liquidity
3. High minimum investment
4. Poor diversification within property section
5. Asset-specific risk
6. Poor diversification across asset categories - cash, fixed rates, residential and commercial property
7. High 'entry fees' when buying - transfer duty (often 3%+), conveyancing fees, time and hassle (4%+)
8. High 'exist fees' when selling - promotional videos, advertising, agent commissions and conveyancing
9. Decrease flexibility
10. Foregone alternatives

**The Ugly**
1. Bursting of property price bubbles and recession
2. Dodgy property developers
3. Home affordability for new home buying

**Rent, borrow and invest over long-term**

Advantages
	Greater flexibility with home
	Tax deduction on interest - home loan is not tax deductible
	Diversification reduces risk
	Can work for Lean FIRE - Shares generate income, house does not

Disadvantages
	Miss out on low interest rates - borrowing for shares is more expensive than buying property
	Life-stages - Home more attractive with kids and when 60+
	Miss out on forced saving
	Speculation

## Sydney property market

**Property prices in the short-term**
Demand:
	Number of buyers in the market
	Perceived shortages of properties
	Fear of missing out
Supply:
	Number of sellers in the market
	Perceived glut of properties
	Fear of not getting out

**Property prices in the long-term**
Demand:
	Birth rates
	Death rates
	Migration and overseas students
	Average income
	Interest rates
	Lives with parents culture
	Savings culture
Supply:
	State government land releases
	Local government density rules
	New home construction
	Availability of credit for developers
	Public transport
	Motorways and roads
	Technology
	Mainly determined by supply-side

**House price indices**
Be careful, these are averages (medians), micro-markets within Sydney and rural areas can be quite different, the quality of the average dwelling has changed. Median house prices don't take into account maintenance costs

### Micro-markets
Prices increase at different rates in different suburbs
Determined by supply and demand factors in that area
'Gentrification' is one factor that affects demand
If areas have large amounts of land being developed or large number of new apartments, then additional supply may result in weak property prices

## Property taxes

### CGT main residence exemption
Main residence is exempt from capital gains tax (gain in price)
	this must be the case to avoid 'losing money' when moving houses
	this makes it an important method for creating and 'storing' wealth
	not a foreign resident
	is on land < 2 hectares
	Main residence for the entire period
	Has not been used to produced assessable income
	not run a business from it

Home office
ATO has made a distinction between:
	A 'place of business' - running and occupancy costs may be an allowable deduction for tax purposes
		CGT main residence exemption is reduced proportionally
	A 'place of convenience' - only running costs are an allowable deduction for tax purposes
		CGT main residence exemption still applies in full

Renting
Charing rent to a family member is a domestic arrange
	the rent received is not assessable for income tax
	it does not affect CGT main residence exemption

Charing rent to a 'border' for a room in your residence
	if you are carrying on a business or renting it out on a platform (airbnb) , rent received is assessable income and a portion of gain in price may be subject to CGT
	If you are recouping costs on a non-commercial basis, rent received is not assessable income and gain in price may not be subject to CGT

**CGT exemption for inherited dwellings**
No tax is normally payable on inheritances of main residence
	this must be the case avoid a perceived death tax on main residence
